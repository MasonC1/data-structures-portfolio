Why does learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matter.

Learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matters because raw algorithms and code cannot completely understand human behavior. It cannot account for factors such as human bias, which can stem from many sources, including culture and how someone was raised. In short, it cannot account for personal opinions or experiences. It can crunch the raw numbers that are involved but not the story behind it. For example, this past NFL season was one of Patrick Mahomes' worst statistical seasons. However, when looking at his raw numbers and performance, it does not account for new coaching, changes on the offensive line, injuries, as well as changes in his receiving core. When we combine the technical science with the social science, we are able to get a more concise reasoning for why things may happen. It makes pattern recognition more understandable. Another thing that we can look at would be the responsibilities that come with looking at data about people. When looking at data about people, we also need to consider privacy. Since privacy is a huge concern in people's lives, especially with the rise of tracking technologies, privacy is a huge thing to look at. You cannot look at or include every piece of data in a person's life, even if it pertains to a topic, because this person may not want to share that information. For example, if analyzing how much the average person spends at the grocery store, a person may not want to share their bank statements so people do not know how much they make; for instance, a person may be willing to share how much they spent but not what they spent it on. One thing with data science that I really want to better understand while taking this course is graphing data. I see all over my timelines sports analysts posting their graphs or charts, some of which are interactive. I would love to understand how to better code these kinds of things. I would also love to better understand methods for filtering data to include things that the average person may not realize. Such as a quarterback's stats in correlation to offensive line protection stats.
